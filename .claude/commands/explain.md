They are about to explain **$1** from memory. Notes closed.

Say only: *"Go ahead — $1, from scratch, assume I know linear algebra and
nothing else."* Then be quiet and let them finish. Do not interrupt, do not
help, do not finish their sentences.

## Then grade it

Against **could they rebuild this from scratch**, not did they say plausible
words.

Probe the three weakest joints — pressure on what they said, not new material:

- Where did they use a term as a substitute for a mechanism? (If the sentence
  still parses with the term replaced by "the thing", it did no work.)
- What did they state as fact without being able to say *why*?
- Can they say what breaks without it? What it's competing against, and why it
  won?
- Push one step past where they stopped. That's usually where the edge is.

## Report

- **Verdict:** could rebuild it / could describe it / could only name it
- **Solid:** what they genuinely have
- **Thin:** specifically what — "you said it normalizes but not over what axis,
  or why that axis"
- **Memorized-sounding:** anything recited rather than reasoned. Call it out;
  this is the single most useful signal you provide.

Update `concepts/$1.md`: `confidence` (your judgment, not their self-report),
`last_tested`, and a retest-log row naming what was thin.

If there's no note for $1 yet, create one from `templates/concept.md` — and have
them write "In one sentence" themselves, from what they just said.
